01 · Quality audits · Amazon · 2021 to 2022

The audit sample behind a quality score

  • Quality 96% → 99%
  • Utilisation 70% → 85%

The problem

Quality sat at 96% on a compliance operations team at Amazon. We checked how the work was sampled for audit before looking at training.

The approach, in outline

We changed how work was sampled for audit and redesigned the performance measures that sat on top of it. Quality went to 99%, and utilisation went from 70% to 85%.

02 · City launch · Swiggy, a food delivery app in India · 2017 to 2019

A new city, from zero orders to 7,000 a day

  • 0 → 7,000 orders a day
  • 900+ delivery partners
  • Just over 4 months
  • Cost per delivery −20%

The problem

Swiggy had no operation in Patna, a state capital in the east of India. The city had to go from zero orders to a working delivery network.

The approach, in outline

I worked out the cost design and the staffing the city needed, and ran the setup and expansion from that. Patna reached 900+ delivery partners and 7,000 orders a day in just over 4 months, and cost per delivery came down 20%.

Try this tomorrowIf you are opening a site or a city, write down the staffing you need at each order level you expect before you hire the first person.

03 · Collections · Simpl, a pay-later checkout in India · 2022 to 2025

Late payments down about half while sales grew

  • Late payments about −50%
  • Sales (GMV) 1.5×
  • Collection cost −20%

The problem

Simpl lets shoppers buy now and pay later. Delinquency (customers paying late or not at all) had to fall while GMV, the value of sales through the product, kept growing.

The approach, in outline

As Operations Lead, I worked with the risk, data science and product teams on late payments. Delinquency came down about 50% while GMV grew 1.5 times. Collection cost fell 20% through a leaner team structure and process fixes.

05 · AI for finance work · my current work

A 10-minute finance entry, now about 3 seconds

  • About 10 min → about 3 s per document
  • Each entry checked after it is written

The problem

A finance entry took about 10 minutes per document by hand. A faster version also had to prove each entry had landed correctly.

The approach, in outline

I built a bulk loader with Claude, an AI assistant. Every document goes through four steps: a dry run on the file, one write, a read-back from the system of record, and a field-by-field match. A mismatch is flagged and never counted as done. Claude wrote most of the code, and I wrote the rules it had to follow.

Try this tomorrowPick one automated write in your work and ask what it checks after it writes. If the answer is "it logs success", add a read-back.
